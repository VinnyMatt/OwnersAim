# Video script — "One model of your whole financial life"

**Purpose:** explain what the tool does and how flexible it is — how much or how little
detail can go in — so a client watching it understands that this is a working model of
their financial ecosystem, built to give them confidence and clarity, not a brochure.

**Format:** 1080p screen recording of the James & Sarah Smith demonstration plan
(`lets-model-it-tool-tour.mp4`, 4 min 10 s, silent). The timecodes below match that
footage exactly, so a voiceover can be recorded straight over it, or the rows can be
read as presenter notes while the video plays. Voiceover pace ≈ 150 words/minute.

---

| TIME | ON SCREEN | VOICEOVER |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Overview** — the plan opens on five ledger figures: net worth today, at retirement, at 95, lifetime tax, first shortfall. The cursor sweeps the lifetime net-worth chart. | This is a financial model of one family's entire life — every account, every year, from today to age ninety-five. One page tells you where they stand: what they're worth now, at retirement, at the end — and whether the plan ever runs short. Everything you're about to see sits behind these five numbers. |
| 0:12 | **About you** — the cursor clicks into James's salary, types **70,000**, and every figure recalculates; then puts it back. Slow scroll through spending, assets and debts. | It starts with the facts: who you are, what you earn, spend, own and owe. And none of it is static — change a salary and the whole model recalculates as you type. You choose the depth: a handful of headline figures is enough to model with, or capture every account, fund and fee — the tool works at whatever level of detail suits the conversation. |
| 0:33 | **Assumptions** — inflation, wage growth and the cash buffer; then the growth-by-asset table, where the S&S ISA rate is nudged from 5.0 to 5.5 and back. Scroll to the year-by-year override grid. | Every assumption is on the table, literally. Inflation, wage growth, and a growth rate for each individual asset, net of its fees. Nothing is hidden in the engine — and if you believe next decade looks different, any rate can be overridden year by year. |
| 0:52 | **Cash flow** — the cursor sweeps the lifetime chart: blue employment, then orange withdrawals, green pensions. A retirement year is clicked; the drawer opens and scrolls through income, tax and every account balance; then closes. | The heart of it: every bar is one year of their life. Blue is earnings; when work stops you can see exactly what replaces it — savings first, in orange, then the State Pension in green. And any year opens up: every pound in, every pound of tax, every closing balance. Nothing in this model is a black box. |
| 1:12 | **Net worth** — the stacked asset chart swept slowly; scroll to the by-year table. | Net worth stacks every asset, year by year — pensions, ISAs, the family home — with debts netted off in the line. You can watch wealth build, shift between pots, and pass down the years. |
| 1:27 | **Businesses** — "+ Add a business" is clicked live; a company appears with turnover, margin, ownership and a valuation chart; scroll through its year-by-year table. | If a client owns a company, it gets its own model — added in one click. Turnover, profit margin, a valuation basis — and the business immediately flows through net worth, tax and the estate. Growth and margin can be set for any single year, so a real trading history fits. |
| 1:47 | **Assets** — the selector switches to James's workplace pension; its chart and year-by-year money-in, growth and money-out table follow. | Then any single asset can be followed through its whole life: what goes in, what it grows by, what's drawn out and when it runs down — including assets that only exist inside a scenario. |
| 2:05 | **Debts** — the mortgage table; **300** typed into the overpayment calculator; the green "repaid sooner" line appears and **Add to plan as what-if** is clicked. Sweep of the balance chart. | Debts amortise properly from their own rates and payments. Here's the flexibility in action: type an overpayment — the calculator shows years saved and interest spared — and one click adds it to the plan as a scenario. Offsetting savings against the mortgage is a dropdown away. |
| 2:30 | **Pensions** — slow scroll through each pension card: allocation bars, fees, the fee-drag comparison, the State Pension section. | The pensions page is deliberately educational. What each pot actually holds, what it costs, and what those quiet fees compound to by retirement. It's also where the model's guardrails live — allowance limits, and warnings if a strategy strays near the tax rules. |
| 2:43 | **Investments** — scroll through the ISA and investment cards and the wrapper comparison. | Investments get the same treatment: what's inside each wrapper, and why the same fund behaves so differently in an ISA, a pension or a taxable account. |
| 2:54 | **Tax** — James's year-by-year tax table; sweep of the tax chart; scroll to Sarah's. | Tax is never a single guess. It's calculated per person, per year — income tax band by band, National Insurance, dividends, capital gains — so every withdrawal and every scenario is taxed the way HMRC would tax it. |
| 3:11 | **Inheritance tax** — hover on a late-plan year; the tooltip itemises every asset in the estate, the nil-rate bands, and the 40% charge. | And the question most families never see coming: inheritance tax, year by year. Hover any bar and the whole calculation opens — every asset counted, the allowances, and the bill. Watching that red bar grow is usually the moment estate planning starts. |
| 3:27 | **What-if** — the scenario list (the overpayment from Debts is already there). "Sarah works to 62" is toggled on; the impact tiles react. **+ Add event** opens the template picker; "Planned withdrawals from a pot" is chosen and added — £12,500 a year from each private pension from 57. | This is where the model becomes a conversation. Every scenario is a switch: retire earlier, buy a property, lose a partner, university fees. Toggle one and the whole plan re-projects against the baseline, instantly. Anything can be added — here, a planned drawdown of twelve and a half thousand a year from each pension, taxed correctly, in seconds. |
| 3:53 | **Cash flow** briefly — the pale-green pension withdrawals now stack beside the State Pension — then back to **Overview**, resting on the five figures. | And every scenario shows up everywhere — there are the pension withdrawals, in the cash flow, taxed and visible. One model, every number connected. As much detail as your life needs, as little as the first conversation wants — so the decisions get made with the whole picture in view. *(beat)* Figures are illustrations built on stated assumptions — not guarantees, and not financial advice. |

**Presenter notes**

- The footage was recorded on the fictional Smith demonstration data; every figure on
  screen is illustrative. If the demo build changes, re-record rather than re-cut —
  the recording is scripted (`record_tour.js`) so a fresh take is one command.
- The visible cursor and click-pulse are baked into the footage.
- Total voiceover is ~600 words over 4:10 — a relaxed read. Each row is written to fit
  its segment with a second or two spare; breathe at the section changes.
- Keep the closing disclaimer — it mirrors the wording in the plan and the PDF report.

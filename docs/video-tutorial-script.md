# Video tutorial script — "Your financial plan, explained in three minutes"

**Format:** screen recording of the plan (James & Sarah Smith test data) with voiceover.
**Target length:** ~2½–3 minutes. Voiceover is roughly 430 words — read at a relaxed pace.
**Prep before recording:** open `clients/testvincent/plan.html` in a full-screen browser
window, Data tab showing, no surplus plan active, the two university events switched on,
all other what-ifs off. Practise the three live interactions (year click, retire-at-60
toggle, overpayment calculator) so they land smoothly.

---

| TIME | ON SCREEN | VOICEOVER |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Slow pan across the **Cash flow** chart, then cut to the plan title. | This is a lifetime financial plan — a working model of a family's money, from today to age ninety-five. Let me show you how it works, using our demonstration clients, James and Sarah. |
| 0:15 | **Data** tab. Cursor moves down the Income table, pauses on a salary, clicks in and types a new figure, presses Enter — charts flick as it recalculates. | Everything starts here: who you are, what you earn, what you spend, what you own and what you owe. And it's all live — change any number, add an income, remove one — and the entire plan recalculates instantly. |
| 0:35 | **Cash flow** tab. Hover slowly left to right — bars lift as the cursor moves. | The heart of the plan is the lifetime cash flow. Every bar is a year of the family's life. Blue is employment income; when James and Sarah retire in 2044 the bars change colour — orange is money drawn from savings, green is the State Pension arriving at sixty-eight. The line is everything going out. |
| 0:55 | **Click 2051/52.** The year drawer slides in; scroll it gently. | And any year can be opened. Here's 2051: every pound in, every pound of tax, every pound spent — and the balance of every account at the year end. Nothing in this plan is a black box. |
| 1:10 | **Net worth** tab, hover a few bars. Then **Assets** tab, pick the pension from the dropdown. | Net worth stacks every asset — pensions, ISAs, the family home — year by year. And on the Assets tab you can follow any single asset through its whole life: what goes in, what it grows by, and what's drawn out. |
| 1:25 | **What-if** tab. Toggle **“Sarah retires at 60”** off and on; the comparison chart and “−£… vs baseline” tiles react. | This is where it becomes a conversation. What if Sarah retires earlier? One switch — and the whole plan re-projects against the dashed baseline. Property purchases, career breaks, inheritances, even the loss of a partner: all modelled in seconds, all reversible. |
| 1:45 | **Debts** tab. Type **500** in the overpayment calculator; the green result line appears. Click “Add to plan as what-if”. | Here's a real example. The Smiths' mortgage runs until James is seventy. Five hundred pounds a month of overpayment? Repaid ten years sooner — seventy-eight thousand pounds of interest saved — and one click adds it to the plan. |
| 2:05 | **Tax** tab, scroll James's table briefly. Then **Inheritance tax** tab, hover one bar so the breakdown tooltip shows. | Tax is calculated properly, per person, per year — James pays about twenty-five thousand pounds this year, and you can see exactly how. Inheritance tax gets the same treatment: hover any year and the whole calculation opens up, including pensions joining the estate from 2027. |
| 2:25 | **Pensions** tab, scroll one card — allocation bar, fee drag line visible. | The Pensions and Investments pages explain what the family actually holds — what it's invested in, what it costs, and what those fees quietly add up to. On James's old pension, that's over twenty thousand pounds by retirement. |
| 2:40 | Click **PDF report**; show the print preview cover and summary page. | And when the conversation's done, one click produces the full report to take away. |
| 2:50 | Fade to the plan title card. | One file. Every number connected. Your plan, played out in front of you — so the decisions get made with the whole picture in view. *(beat)* Figures shown are illustrations based on stated assumptions, not guarantees, and not financial advice. |

---

**Presenter notes**

- The hover interactions are the star — move the cursor slowly so the Voyant-style bar
  lift reads on camera.
- All figures quoted are from the Smith test build: retirement 2044/45, State Pension
  age 68, mortgage repaid 2052/53 (James 70) at £1,500/month, £500/month overpayment →
  repaid 2042/43 saving ~£78,000, James's year-one tax ≈ £25,000, old-pension fee drag
  ≈ £23,000, IHT exposure beginning 2027/28. Re-check on the recording build if data
  changes.
- Keep the closing disclaimer in — it mirrors the wording in the PDF report.

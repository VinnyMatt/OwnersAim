# CII Financial Planning Foundations — Methodology Reference

**Purpose:** This document distils the syllabus structure of the Chartered Insurance Institute (CII)
Diploma in Regulated Financial Planning (R01–R06) and Advanced Diploma in Financial Planning
(AF units and related J0 units) into a *practical planning methodology*. It is the foundational
reference a plan-generation skill should read before producing any client financial plan.

**Scope note:** This is an educational/illustrative planning framework, **not regulated financial
advice**. Specific tax rates, allowances and thresholds change every UK tax year — always verify
current-year figures against GOV.UK/HMRC before quoting numbers in an output. Where figures appear
below they are indicative of the *mechanism*, not a substitute for current data.

*Last researched: 25 August 2026.*

## 1. The CII qualifications and unit map

### 1.1 Diploma in Regulated Financial Planning (RQF Level 4)

The Level 4 Diploma is the FCA-recognised benchmark qualification for UK retail investment
advisers. It comprises six compulsory units
([CII qualification page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/diploma-in-regulated-financial-planning-qualification/),
[qualification specification PDF](https://www.cii.co.uk/media/ceql1jyb/qspec-diploma-in-regulated-financial-planning.pdf)):

| Unit | Title | Credits | Assessment | One-line description |
|------|-------|---------|------------|----------------------|
| R01 | Financial Services, Regulation and Ethics | 20 | MCQ exam | UK regulatory framework (FCA/PRA), consumer protection, adviser responsibilities, legal concepts and the CII Code of Ethics |
| R02 | Investment Principles and Risk | 20 | MCQ exam | Asset classes, investment products, risk types, investment theory, macro-economics and the investment advice process |
| R03 | Personal Taxation | 10 | MCQ exam | Income tax, NICs, CGT, IHT basics, residence/domicile, taxation of investments and basic tax planning |
| R04 | Pensions and Retirement Planning | 10 | MCQ exam | Pension tax regime, DB vs DC schemes, state pension, retirement options and pension planning considerations |
| R05 | Financial Protection | 10 | MCQ exam | Life assurance, income protection, critical illness, business protection, and matching protection products to client needs |
| R06 | Financial Planning Practice | 30 | Written case-study exam | Capstone: applying the whole financial planning process to client scenarios (fact-find → analysis → recommendations → review) |

R06's learning outcomes are the clearest statement of the CII's planning process: (1) obtain
appropriate client information and understand clients' needs, wants, values and risk profile;
(2) synthesise client information and subjective factors to form the basis for planning
assumptions and decisions; (3) analyse the client's situation and the advantages/disadvantages of
appropriate options; (4) formulate and communicate suitable recommendations; (5) review
([R06 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/unit-financial-planning-practice-r06/),
[Brand Financial Training summary](https://brandft.co.uk/cii-r06-ro6-financial-planning-practice/)).

Relevant Level 4 "J0" Diploma in Financial Planning units often used for credits
([Brand Financial Training routes overview](https://brandft.co.uk/many-routes-to-chartered/),

- **J02 Trusts** — trust law, wills, executorship, bankruptcy, life policies in trust.
- **J05 Pension Income Options** — crystallisation rules; features, tax treatment and risks of annuities, scheme pensions, drawdown/flexible benefits and phased retirement.

- **J10 Discretionary Investment Management** — securities analysis and portfolio recommendations.
- **J12 Securities Advice and Dealing** — direct securities, FX, warrants, dealing.

**Planning-tool takeaway:** the CII curriculum defines seven practical domains a complete plan
must address: the planning process itself, tax, investments, pensions/retirement income,
protection, estate/IHT, and (cutting across all of them) cash-flow modelling and assumptions.
Sections 2–8 distil each into methodology.

## 2. The financial planning process (R06 / AF5)

The CII six-step process the tool should follow for every plan:

1. **Establish the relationship and scope.** State what the output is (illustrative planning
   analysis), what it is not (regulated advice), and the basis of any assumptions.
2. **Gather data (fact-find) — hard and soft facts.**
   - *Hard facts:* ages, family, health, employment, income, expenditure, assets, liabilities,
     pensions (type, value, contributions), protection policies, wills/LPAs, tax position.
   - *Soft facts:* goals, values, priorities, attitudes, concerns, expected retirement age,
     legacy wishes. AF5/R06 stress that soft facts drive suitability as much as hard facts.
   - Flag missing information explicitly rather than silently assuming it.
3. **Identify and quantify objectives.** Convert vague wants into SMART objectives with a target
   amount, target date and priority ranking (e.g. "retire at 62 on £35,000 p.a. in today's
   money"). Distinguish needs (essential expenditure, protection gaps) from wants (lifestyle) and
   aspirations (legacy).
4. **Analyse the current position against objectives.**
   - Net worth statement (assets − liabilities) and cash-flow statement (income − expenditure).
   - Emergency fund adequacy (conventionally 3–6 months' essential expenditure in cash).
   - Debt position: prioritise expensive unsecured debt before investing.
   - Risk profile: assess **attitude to risk**, **capacity for loss** (can they afford a fall
     without lifestyle damage?) and **need to take risk** (required return to hit the goal) as
     three separate dimensions; the recommendation is bounded by the lowest of the three.
   - Identify shortfalls/surpluses per objective, in today's money.
5. **Formulate and present recommendations.** For each objective: option analysis (advantages and
   disadvantages of realistic alternatives, including "do nothing"), the recommended action, and
   *reasons why* linked back to the client's stated objectives and risk profile. Explain technical
   terms plainly, state features, benefits, drawbacks, costs and risks, and the consequences of
   the proposed action (AF5 learning outcomes).
6. **Implement, then review.** A plan is a snapshot: build in a review cycle (at least annually
   and on life events — marriage, divorce, birth, death, inheritance, job change, legislation
   change). Document any client-requested changes to the plan.

**Ordering heuristic (the classic planning hierarchy):** protect first (emergency fund + insurance
against death/illness), clear expensive debt, then save/invest for goals shortest-to-longest term,
using tax wrappers efficiently, then estate planning on top.

Sources: [R06 syllabus/unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/unit-financial-planning-practice-r06/),
[AF5 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/advanced-diploma-in-financial-planning-qualification/unit-financial-planning-process-af5/).

## 3. Personal taxation planning (R03 / AF1)

Methodology, not rates — verify all figures for the tax year in force:

- **Compute before you plan.** Follow the statutory income tax order: total income → deduct
  allowable reliefs → personal allowance (tapered above the income limit) → tax non-savings
  income, then savings income (starting rate band + personal savings allowance), then dividend
  income (dividend allowance), each at the appropriate band. Scottish taxpayers use Scottish
  bands for non-savings income.
- **Use allowances before products.** Each individual has, per tax year: personal allowance,
  personal savings allowance, dividend allowance, CGT annual exempt amount, ISA allowance,
  pension annual allowance. A couple has two of everything — model inter-spouse transfers
  (no-gain/no-loss for CGT, unlimited IHT-exempt) to use both sets.
- **Watch effective-rate cliff edges.** The personal-allowance taper (60% effective band above
  £100k), child benefit high-income charge, and loss of savings/dividend allowances at higher
  bands make **pension contributions and Gift Aid** (which extend the basic-rate band and reduce
  adjusted net income) disproportionately valuable — always test them when income sits near a
  threshold.
- **Wrapper order for investors (general heuristic):** pension (relief on the way in) and ISA
  (tax-free on the way out) first; then use CGT exempt amount and dividend/savings allowances on
  unwrapped holdings; investment bonds and other structures only where a specific need exists
  (e.g. 5% tax-deferred withdrawals, assignment planning, trustee investment).
- **CGT planning:** use the annual exempt amount each year ("use it or lose it"), realise gains
  gradually, offset losses, consider spousal transfers before disposal, and be aware of the
  30-day/bed-and-breakfast matching rules. Note CGT is generally rebased on death.
- **Residence and domicile** (AF1): the statutory residence test and (since April 2025) the
  residence-based regime for foreign income and gains determine UK exposure — flag, don't model,
  for internationally mobile clients; recommend specialist advice.
- **Document the tax basis.** Every plan output should state the tax year assumed and that tax
  treatment depends on individual circumstances and may change.

Sources: [AF1 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/unit-personal-tax-and-trust-planning-af1/),
[R03 exam guide overview](https://exams.academy/insights/cii-r03-personal-taxation-exam-guide/).

## 4. Investment planning (R02 / AF4 / J10)

- **Objectives before products.** Define for each goal: time horizon, required return, liquidity
  need, income vs growth, ethical/ESG preferences, and tax position. Money needed within ~5 years
  should generally stay in cash/near-cash, not markets.
- **Risk profiling is three-dimensional** (R02/AF4): attitude to risk (psychometric willingness),
  capacity for loss (objective ability to absorb falls — driven by wealth, secured income, time
  horizon and flexibility), and need for risk (return required to meet the objective). Reconcile
  conflicts explicitly: e.g. high attitude but low capacity → constrain risk; low attitude but
  high need → either lower the goal, save more, extend the horizon, or accept more risk — state
  the trade-off, don't hide it.
- **Asset classes and their long-run behaviour:** cash (capital-secure nominally, loses to
  inflation), fixed interest — gilts, index-linked gilts, corporate bonds (interest-rate,
  inflation and credit risk), equities (highest long-run real return, highest volatility),
  property (income + illiquidity), alternatives/commodities (diversifiers). Model **correlation**
  between classes; diversification across imperfectly correlated assets is the only free lunch
  (Modern Portfolio Theory / efficient frontier — AF4).
- **Asset allocation drives outcomes.** Build portfolios top-down from a strategic asset
  allocation matched to the risk profile and horizon; security/fund selection is secondary.
  Rebalance periodically back to target weights (disciplined sell-high/buy-low; controls risk
  drift).
- **Real vs nominal returns — always show both.** State expected returns gross, then net of
  charges, then net of inflation. A plan quoting only nominal growth materially overstates future
  purchasing power. Long-horizon goals should be expressed and tracked **in today's money**.
- **Costs compound.** Model ongoing charges (OCF, platform, advice) explicitly; a 1% p.a. charge
  difference compounds to a large terminal-value difference over 20–30 years.
- **Risk types to name in analysis** (R02): capital/market risk, inflation risk, interest-rate
  risk, credit/default risk, currency risk, liquidity risk, shortfall risk (the risk of *not*
  taking enough risk), and sequencing risk (see §5).
- **Performance measurement** (AF4): compare against an appropriate benchmark; distinguish
  money-weighted vs time-weighted returns; use risk-adjusted measures (Sharpe, alpha) rather than
  raw return alone. Past performance is not a guide to future performance — never extrapolate a
  specific recent pattern of returns (echoed by the FCA's cash-flow modelling guidance, §8).

Sources: [R02 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/unit-investment-principles-and-risk-r02/),
[AF4 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/advanced-diploma-in-financial-planning-qualification/unit-investment-planning-af4/).

## 5. Pension and retirement income planning (R04 / AF7 / AF8 / J05)

### Accumulation

- **Quantify the target first:** required retirement income in today's money, minus secured
  income (State Pension — check forecast via GOV.UK; DB pensions; annuities), equals the gap the
  fund must cover. Work back to required fund (gap ÷ sustainable withdrawal rate, or annuity cost)
  and then to required contributions given horizon and assumed real return.
- **Pension tax mechanics to exploit:** tax relief at marginal rate on personal contributions
  (within annual allowance and 100%-of-relevant-earnings limits); employer contributions and
  salary sacrifice (NI saving); carry-forward of unused annual allowance; the **tapered annual
  allowance** for high earners and the **MPAA** once flexible benefits have been drawn — both must
  be checked before recommending contributions. Since abolition of the lifetime allowance, model
  the lump-sum allowance (LSA) and lump-sum & death benefit allowance (LSDBA) limits on tax-free
  cash instead.
- **Never advise transferring out of DB/safeguarded benefits by default** (AF7): guaranteed,
  inflation-linked, spouse-protected income is the benchmark; the FCA's starting assumption is
  that a transfer is unsuitable. A planning tool should treat DB pensions as secured income and
  flag any transfer question as requiring specialist regulated advice.

### Decumulation (AF8 / J05)

- **Match income layers to needs:** essential expenditure ideally covered by secured income
  (State Pension, DB, annuity); discretionary expenditure from flexible drawdown; contingency
  from accessible capital. Consider annuitising more with age or on health change (enhanced
  annuities).
- **The main options** (J05): scheme pension, lifetime annuity (level/escalating, single/joint,
  guarantees), flexi-access drawdown, UFPLS, phased retirement — compare on flexibility, death
  benefits, tax, and longevity/investment risk transfer.
- **Sustainable withdrawal:** a guide rate of roughly 3–4% of the initial fund, inflation-adjusted,
  is the conventional planning heuristic for a 30-year retirement, but sustainability must be
  *tested*, not assumed (see §8): sequencing risk means poor early returns plus fixed withdrawals
  can deplete a fund even when average returns look adequate. Mitigants: cash buffer (1–3 years'
  withdrawals), dynamic/guardrail withdrawal rules, natural yield, partial annuitisation.
- **Plan beyond average life expectancy.** The FCA criticises plans that stop at life expectancy;
  model to at least age 95–100 and state the longevity assumption
  ([FCA retirement income advice review](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/retirement-income-advice-good-practice-areas-improvement)).
- **Later life:** integrate potential care costs, loss of one partner's income (survivor pension
  levels, single person's higher per-capita costs), and cognitive decline (recommend LPAs).

Sources: [AF7 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/advanced-diploma-in-financial-planning-qualification/unit-pension-transfers-af7/),
[AF8 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/unit-retirement-income-planning-af8/),
[J05 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/diploma-in-financial-planning-qualification/unit-pension-income-options-j05/).

## 6. Protection planning (R05)

Protection comes **first** in the planning hierarchy — investment plans built on unprotected
households fail on death or illness.

- **Needs analysis method:** for each life event (death, long-term illness/incapacity, critical
  illness), quantify: (a) capital needs — repay mortgage/debts, immediate expenses, education
  funds; (b) income needs — replacement of net income until dependency ends, capitalised or
  covered by family income benefit. Subtract existing cover (employer death-in-service, group IP,
  state benefits — noting how limited these are) to get the **gap**.
- **Product-to-need mapping:**
  - *Level/decreasing term assurance* — repayment of debt, family protection for a defined term
    (decreasing term matches a repayment mortgage cheaply).
  - *Family income benefit* — income replacement for dependants; cheapest way to buy income-shaped
    cover.
  - *Whole of life* — permanent needs, notably IHT liability funding (written in trust).
  - *Income protection (IP)* — long-term incapacity income to retirement age; usually the most
    undervalued need for working-age clients; check deferred period against sick pay and emergency
    fund; benefit paid tax-free if personally funded.
  - *Critical illness cover* — lump sum on diagnosis of specified conditions; complements, not
    replaces, IP.
  - *Business protection* — key person, shareholder/partnership protection, relevant life plans.
- **Priorities:** income protection and life cover for dependants generally rank above CIC;
  single people with no dependants may need only IP.
- **Structure matters:** write life policies **in trust** (or use death-in-service nominations) so
  proceeds fall outside the estate for IHT and pay quickly without probate. Review cover on every
  life event.

Source: [R05 unit page and exam guide](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/diploma-in-regulated-financial-planning-qualification/) (unit list),
[BTS R05 overview](https://www.bespoketrainingsolutions.com/what-are-the-r0-exams-bts/).

## 7. Estate and IHT planning (R03 / AF1 / J02)

- **Baseline first:** compute the estate (worldwide assets for UK-domiciled/long-term residents,
  less liabilities), apply the nil-rate band (NRB), residence nil-rate band (RNRB — only against a
  home left to direct descendants, tapered for estates above the threshold), spouse exemption and
  transferable unused NRB/RNRB between spouses. IHT at 40% on the excess (36% where ≥10% of the
  net estate goes to charity). State the resulting liability in the plan.
- **Order of mitigation (least to most invasive):**
  1. **Wills and ownership** — ensure valid, current wills (intestacy rarely matches wishes);
     check property ownership (joint tenants vs tenants in common); LPAs for incapacity.
  2. **Exemptions** — annual gift exemption, small gifts, gifts on marriage, spouse and charity
     exemptions, and **normal expenditure out of income** (unlimited if habitual, from income,
     leaving lifestyle intact — document it).
  3. **Lifetime gifting** — PETs falling out of account after 7 years (taper relief on tax after
     3); CLTs into trust within the NRB. Model the 7-year clock and warn about gifts with
     reservation of benefit and the pre-owned assets rules.
  4. **Trusts** (J02/AF1) — discretionary, bare, interest-in-possession; and packaged solutions
     (loan trusts, discounted gift trusts) that balance access with IHT saving; note
     relevant-property regime charges (entry/periodic/exit).
  5. **Reliefs** — business relief / agricultural relief on qualifying assets (rules and caps
     changed materially from April 2026 — verify current limits).
  6. **Insurance** — whole-of-life in trust to fund the residual liability rather than remove it.
- **Pensions and IHT:** death benefits have historically sat outside the estate; from April 2027
  unused pension funds are due to come within IHT — a planning tool must check the current rule
  before asserting pensions are IHT-free, and model both regimes near the boundary.
- **Never gift away security:** capacity-for-loss logic applies — mitigation must not compromise
  the donor's own funded retirement and care contingency (link to the cash-flow model, §8).

Source: [AF1 unit page](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/qualifications/unit-personal-tax-and-trust-planning-af1/).

## 8. Cash-flow modelling best practice

The FCA's March 2024 guidance on cash-flow modelling for retirement-related advice is the de facto
standard and should govern the tool's engine
([FCA: Undertaking cashflow modelling](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/undertaking-cashflow-modelling-demonstrate-suitability-retirement-related-advice)):

1. **Accurate inputs.** Use real client data (verified incomes, actual plan values, real
   expenditure split essential/discretionary); flag estimates as estimates.
2. **Justifiable return assumptions.** Returns must align with the actual/proposed asset
   allocation and be net of all charges; do not base them on a specific pattern of past returns.
   Constant (deterministic) rates are acceptable **only with appropriate stress testing**.
   Maintain a sensible differential between asset-class returns and inflation (real returns);
   review assumptions regularly against economic conditions.
3. **Model in today's money.** Express future income and expenditure consistently in real terms
   so clients can judge adequacy; if nominal figures are shown, label them clearly and show the
   real equivalent.
4. **Plan beyond average life expectancy** — to age 95–100, and show the effect of living longer.
5. **Stress test — the FCA's four illustrative stresses:**
   - a rare-but-feasible **market crash at the start of withdrawals** (sequencing risk);
   - **reduced real returns** throughout, showing when the fund depletes;
   - for stochastic models, show **lower-percentile outcomes**, not just the median;
   - **higher withdrawals** than planned, showing accelerated depletion.
   Add scenario/what-if analysis for life events: early retirement, death of a partner, care
   costs, higher inflation, lower state pension age changes.
6. **Output must aid understanding:** show the base case and stressed cases side by side; a chart
   of fund value / income vs need over time; and a clear statement of every assumption used.

Sources also: [FCA retirement income advice good/poor practice](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/retirement-income-advice-good-practice-areas-improvement),
[Synaptic on cash-flow modelling](https://www.synaptic.co.uk/insights/connection/2024-q4/cash-flow-modelling).

## 9. Modelling principles for the tool

### 9.1 Prudent assumption-setting

- Prefer **modest real-return assumptions** by asset class (e.g. cash ≈ 0% real or slightly
  negative; bonds ≈ 0–1% real; diversified equities ≈ 3–5% real, long run), blended by the
  portfolio's asset allocation and reduced by total charges. State the source/basis of each rate.
- Use a single, explicit **inflation assumption** (CPI-linked, commonly 2–2.5% for general prices;
  consider higher rates for earnings and specific costs such as education or care).
- Use **current tax-year rates and thresholds**, name the tax year, and assume current legislation
  continues unless a change is already enacted (then model the enacted change from its start
  date).
- Round prudently: understate growth, overstate costs and longevity, when in doubt.

### 9.2 Real vs nominal

Default all goal-tracking, income-adequacy and shortfall figures to **today's money**. Show
nominal figures only alongside their real equivalents, clearly labelled. Never mix real
expenditure with nominal returns in the same projection.

### 9.3 Sensitivity and stress testing

Every projection should ship with, at minimum: (a) base case; (b) low-return case (e.g. −1% to
−2% p.a. real); (c) early-crash case (e.g. −20–30% portfolio fall in year 1 of drawdown);
(d) high-inflation case; (e) longevity case (live 5+ years beyond base). Report the first year of
shortfall/depletion in each.

### 9.4 Documenting assumptions

Each generated plan must include an **Assumptions appendix** listing: tax year; inflation;
gross and net return per asset class/portfolio; charges; earnings growth; state pension amount
and commencement age; annuity basis if used; mortality horizon; contribution patterns; and any
client-specific estimates, each with its basis. If an input was missing and defaulted, say so.

### 9.5 Standard disclaimer language (illustrative, non-advice outputs)

Include wording to this effect on every plan:

> This document is an illustrative financial planning analysis produced for information and
> educational purposes only. It does not constitute financial advice, a personal recommendation,
> tax advice or legal advice, and no action should be taken on the basis of this document alone.
> The projections shown are illustrations based on the stated assumptions; they are not
> guarantees, forecasts or minimum/maximum outcomes, and actual results will differ. The value of
> investments can fall as well as rise, and you may get back less than you invest. Past
> performance is not a reliable indicator of future performance. Levels, bases and reliefs of
> taxation depend on individual circumstances and may change. Figures are shown in today's money
> unless stated otherwise, using the assumptions listed in the appendix. A pension is a long-term
> investment; transferring out of a defined benefit pension is unlikely to be in most people's
> best interests and requires regulated advice. Before acting on any matter covered here you
> should consider seeking advice from a financial adviser authorised and regulated by the
> Financial Conduct Authority (register.fca.org.uk). If you are unsure about pension options, the
> government's free MoneyHelper / Pension Wise guidance service is available.

### 9.6 Suitability logic checks (pre-output validation)

Before emitting a plan, verify: emergency fund addressed before investment recommendations;
protection gaps flagged before wealth-building; no equity exposure recommended for sub-3–5-year
goals; recommended risk level ≤ min(attitude, capacity); withdrawals tested for sustainability;
DB benefits treated as secured income, not transfer candidates; all allowances (ISA, pension AA,
CGT AEA) checked for both members of a couple; assumptions appendix and disclaimer present.

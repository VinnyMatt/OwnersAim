## 1. Income tax (England, Wales & Northern Ireland)

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Personal allowance | £12,570 | same |
| Basic rate | 20% on £12,571 – £50,270 (band £37,700) | same |
| Higher rate | 40% on £50,271 – £125,140 | same |
| Additional rate | 45% above £125,140 | same |
| Personal allowance taper | −£1 per £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000; nil at £125,140 | same |
| Marriage allowance | Transfer £1,260 of PA to spouse/civil partner (saves up to £252) | same |

**How it works:** Non-savings, non-dividend income above the personal allowance is taxed in slices at 20/40/45%. The £100k–£125,140 taper creates an effective ~60% marginal rate. Marriage allowance requires the transferor to be a non-taxpayer and the recipient a basic-rate taxpayer. All thresholds frozen to 5 April 2031 (Autumn Budget 2025 extended the freeze from 2028); indexation resumes 2031/32. Note announced future change: from April 2027 **savings** income rates rise to 22/42/47% and **property** income gets its own 22/42/47% rates — model these for plans crossing 2027/28.

## 2. National Insurance

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Class 1 employee (primary) main rate | 8% between primary threshold and UEL | same |
| Class 1 employee rate above UEL | 2% | same |
| Primary threshold | £12,570/yr (£242/wk) | same |
| Upper earnings limit (UEL) | £50,270/yr (£967/wk) | same |
| Lower earnings limit (LEL) | £6,708/yr | £6,500/yr |
| Employer (secondary) rate | 15% above secondary threshold | same |
| Secondary threshold | £5,000/yr | same |
| Employment allowance | £10,500 (eligible employers, offset vs employer NI) | same |
| Class 4 self-employed | 6% on profits £12,570 – £50,270; 2% above | same |
| Class 2 | Abolished as a charge; treated as paid if profits ≥ small profits threshold £7,105; voluntary £3.65/wk below | SPT £6,845; voluntary £3.50/wk |

**How it works:** Employee NI is calculated per pay period on earnings between the primary threshold and UEL at 8%, then 2% above. Employers pay 15% on everything above £5,000/yr per employee (no upper limit); the employment allowance wipes up to £10,500 of the total employer bill for eligible businesses. Self-employed pay Class 4 with their Self Assessment; Class 2 is credited automatically above the SPT (protects State Pension record). Earnings between LEL and primary threshold incur no NI but earn NI credits. Note: from 6 April 2029 pension salary-sacrifice amounts above £2,000/yr become NI-able (both employee and employer).

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027 — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via FreeAgent, Moorepay, Crunch).

## 3. Dividend tax

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Dividend allowance | £500 | same |
| Basic rate (ordinary) | **10.75%** | 8.75% |
| Higher rate (upper) | **35.75%** | 33.75% |
| Additional rate | 39.35% | same |

**How it works:** Dividends are taxed as the top slice of income (after non-savings and savings income). The first £500 is taxed at 0% but still uses up the band it falls in. Rates rose 2 percentage points at basic and higher rates from 6 April 2026 (Autumn Budget 2025); additional rate and allowance unchanged. Dividend rates are UK-wide (including Scotland).

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-dividends — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via ICAEW, Evelyn Partners, Deloitte tax tables 2026/27).

## 4. Savings income

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Personal savings allowance — basic rate taxpayer | £1,000 | same |
| Personal savings allowance — higher rate | £500 | same |
| Personal savings allowance — additional rate | £0 | same |
| Starting rate for savings | 0% on up to £5,000 of interest | same |

**How it works:** The starting rate band sits directly above the personal allowance: it is reduced £1-for-£1 by non-savings income above £12,570 and is fully lost once non-savings income exceeds £17,570. The PSA then applies on top (a 0% band, determined by the taxpayer's highest marginal rate). Interest above these is taxed at the taxpayer's marginal rate in 2026/27 — but from April 2027 savings income will be taxed at dedicated rates of 22% / 42% / 47%. The £5,000 starting rate band is frozen to 2030/31.

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/apply-tax-free-interest-on-savings — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via LITRG, taxesdoneright.co.uk, ICAEW Budget coverage).

## 5. Capital Gains Tax

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Annual exempt amount | £3,000 (£1,500 for most trusts) | same |
| Rate within basic-rate band | 18% | same |
| Rate above basic-rate band | 24% | same |
| Residential property rates | Same 18% / 24% (aligned since 30 Oct 2024) | same |
| Business Asset Disposal Relief rate | **18%** | 14% |
| BADR lifetime limit | £1,000,000 of qualifying gains | same |

**How it works:** Gains net of losses and the £3,000 AEA are added on top of taxable income: the part filling the remaining basic-rate band is taxed at 18%, the rest at 24%. Residential property no longer has separate higher rates (same 18/24), but sales still require a 60-day CGT return and payment. BADR taxes qualifying business disposals at 18% (final step of the phased rise from 10%→14%→18%) up to a £1m lifetime cap. No CGT on death (assets rebase); ISA/pension gains exempt.

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax/rates — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via uktax.tools, taxfly.co.uk, Deloitte tax tables 2026/27).

## 6. ISAs

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Adult ISA overall subscription limit | £20,000 | same |
| Lifetime ISA limit (within the £20,000) | £4,000 + 25% government bonus (max £1,000/yr) | same |
| LISA unauthorised-withdrawal penalty | 25% of amount withdrawn | same |
| LISA property price cap | £450,000 | same |
| Junior ISA | £9,000 | same |

**How it works:** No tax on interest, dividends or gains inside any ISA. The £20,000 can be split across cash, stocks & shares, innovative finance and LISA (LISA capped at £4,000; contributions allowed age 18–50, account opened before 40). LISA withdrawals are penalty-free only for a first home (≤£450k) or from age 60; otherwise 25% is charged (which claws back the bonus plus ~6.25% of own money). **Announced changes:** from 6 April 2027 the cash-ISA portion is capped at £12,000 for under-65s (over-65s keep £20,000 in cash; overall £20,000 unchanged); a consultation is underway to replace the LISA with a "First-Time Buyer ISA" around April 2028.

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/individual-savings-accounts — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via moneyfactscompare.co.uk, YBS, St. James's Place, MSE).

## 7. Pensions

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Annual allowance (AA) | £60,000 | same |
| Tapered AA — threshold income trigger | £200,000 | same |
| Tapered AA — adjusted income trigger | £260,000 | same |
| Taper mechanics | AA reduced £1 per £2 of adjusted income over £260,000; floor £10,000 (fully tapered at £360,000) | same |
| Money Purchase Annual Allowance (MPAA) | £10,000 | same |
| Carry forward | Unused AA from previous 3 tax years (2023/24–2025/26), current year first; must have been a scheme member in those years | same |
| Lump Sum Allowance (LSA) | £268,275 | same |
| Lump Sum & Death Benefit Allowance (LSDBA) | £1,073,100 | same |
| Full new State Pension | **£241.30/wk (£12,547.60/yr)** | £230.25/wk |
| Full basic (old) State Pension | **£184.90/wk** | £176.45/wk |

**How it works:** Contributions get relief at marginal rate. Relief at source (personal/SIPP and most auto-enrol GPPs): pay net, provider adds 20%, higher/additional (and Scottish intermediate+) taxpayers claim the rest via Self Assessment/tax code. Net pay: contribution deducted pre-tax, full relief automatic. Personal contributions capped at 100% of relevant UK earnings (or £3,600 gross if lower) for relief; AA (employer + employee + tax relief combined) breaches trigger an AA charge at marginal rate. MPAA replaces the AA for money-purchase savings once a DC pension is flexibly accessed (drawdown income, UFPLS) and kills carry forward for DC. Since LTA abolition (6 Apr 2024) there is no lifetime cap on the fund — only on tax-free lump sums: the LSA caps lifetime tax-free cash (usually 25% of each crystallisation) at £268,275; the LSDBA caps combined lifetime + death tax-free lump sums at £1,073,100 (higher if transitional/protections apply). State Pension rose 4.8% in April 2026 under the triple lock (highest of average earnings growth, CPI, 2.5% — earnings won); full new rate needs 35 qualifying NI years (minimum 10). **Announced:** unused pension funds enter the IHT estate from 6 April 2027 (see §9); salary-sacrifice NI exemption capped at £2,000/yr from April 2029.

**Tax-free cash recycling (PTM133810):** HMRC treats a pension commencement lump sum as an **unauthorised member payment** (40% charge on the individual plus a possible 15% surcharge — up to 55%) when ALL of these are met: (1) the tax-free cash, together with any other tax-free cash taken in the previous 12 months, exceeds **£7,500**; (2) because of the lump sum, contributions to registered pension schemes (by the individual, their employer, or anyone on their behalf) are **significantly greater** than otherwise expected — HMRC's yardstick is more than 30% above the established pattern; (3) the **cumulative** additional contributions across the tax year of the lump sum and the **two tax years either side** (a five-tax-year window) exceed **30% of the lump sum**; and (4) the recycling was **pre-planned**. Recycling of taxable drawdown *income* back into contributions is not caught by this rule (the MPAA is the control there). All conditions must be met — failing any one keeps the lump sum authorised.

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-your-private-pension/annual-allowance, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-the-rules-around-individual-lump-sum-allowances, https://www.gov.uk/new-state-pension — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via abrdn/Aberdeen adviser techzone, Royal London, MoneyHelper, MSE, Fidelity). Recycling rule: HMRC Pensions Tax Manual PTM133810 (gov.uk blocked by the working environment's egress proxy on 28 Aug 2026 — figures cross-verified via Quilter, M&G Wealth, Aegon and Hargreaves Lansdown adviser/technical pages, which agree on all five conditions and the charge rates).

## 8. Child Benefit & HICBC

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Eldest/only child | £27.05/wk (£1,406.60/yr) | £26.05/wk |
| Each additional child | £17.90/wk (£930.80/yr) | £17.25/wk |
| HICBC start threshold | £60,000 adjusted net income | same |
| HICBC full clawback | £80,000 | same |
| Taper | 1% of the benefit repaid per £200 over £60,000 | same |

**How it works:** Paid 4-weekly, tax-free unless the higher-earning partner's adjusted net income (income minus gross pension contributions and gift aid) exceeds £60,000; the charge claws back 1% per £200 above that, so 100% at £80,000. The charge is on the higher earner regardless of who claims. Since 2025/26 it can be collected via PAYE instead of Self Assessment. Claiming (even opting out of payments) still earns NI credits toward the State Pension for a parent not working. Pension contributions reduce adjusted net income and can restore the benefit.

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/child-benefit-rates and https://www.gov.uk/child-benefit-tax-charge — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via uktax.tools, Commons Library CBP-8631, insighthq.uk).

## 9. Inheritance tax

| Item | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Nil-rate band (NRB) | £325,000 | same |
| Residence nil-rate band (RNRB) | £175,000 | same |
| RNRB taper | −£1 per £2 of estate value over £2,000,000 | same |
| Rate on death | 40% (36% if ≥10% of net estate left to charity) | same |
| Lifetime rate (chargeable transfers, e.g. into most trusts) | 20% | same |
| Spouse/civil partner exemption | Unlimited (capped at £325,000 to a spouse who is not UK long-term resident, unless they elect in) | same |
| Annual gift exemption | £3,000/yr (one prior year's unused amount can be carried forward) | same |
| Small gifts | £250 per recipient per year (not combinable with other exemptions for same person) | same |
| Wedding gifts | £5,000 parent / £2,500 grandparent / £1,000 anyone else | same |
| Gifts from surplus income | Unlimited if regular, from income, and leaving normal standard of living intact | same |
| APR/BPR 100% relief cap | **£2.5m combined (50% relief above); transferable between spouses; refreshes every 7 yrs** (from 6 Apr 2026; originally announced as £1m, raised Dec 2025) | uncapped 100% |

**PETs & taper relief:** outright gifts to individuals are Potentially Exempt Transfers — fully exempt if the donor survives 7 years. If death occurs within 7 years, the gift uses NRB first (oldest gifts first); tax **on the gift** (only where gifts exceed the NRB) is reduced by taper relief: death in years 0–3: 0% reduction (full 40%); 3–4 yrs: 20% off (32%); 4–5: 40% (24%); 5–6: 60% (16%); 6–7: 80% (8%).

**How it works:** Estate above the available NRB (+RNRB where a home passes to direct descendants) is taxed at 40%. Unused NRB/RNRB transfers to a surviving spouse (up to £1m combined for a couple with a qualifying home). RNRB tapers away for estates over £2m (gone at £2.35m per person). NRB, RNRB and the £2m taper threshold are frozen to 5 April 2031. **Confirmed future change:** from **6 April 2027**, unused pension funds and most pension death benefits are included in the estate for IHT (legislated in Finance Act; death-in-service benefits from registered schemes excluded) — a major planning consideration for DC pots currently outside the estate.

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via Commons Library CBP-10181, Saffery, ATT, Legal & General adviser, abrdn techzone).

## 10. Other

### Stamp Duty Land Tax — residential, England & NI

| Band (main residence) | Rate 2026/27 |
|---|---|
| Up to £125,000 | 0% |
| £125,001 – £250,000 | 2% |
| £250,001 – £925,000 | 5% |
| £925,001 – £1,500,000 | 10% |
| Over £1,500,000 | 12% |

- Charged on a slice basis. Unchanged from 2025/26 (bands reverted to these levels on 1 April 2025).
- **Additional property surcharge:** +5% on every band (second homes / buy-to-let).
- **First-time buyers:** 0% to £300,000, 5% on £300,001–£500,000; no relief above £500,000.
- Scotland uses LBTT and Wales LTT — different bands, not covered here.
- Announced for the future: a "high value council tax surcharge" on homes over £2m from April 2028 (not an SDLT change).

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates — accessed 25 Aug 2026 (verified via MoneyHelper, propertypassport.uk).
